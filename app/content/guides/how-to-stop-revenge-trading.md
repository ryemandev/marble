Revenge trading is taking a trade to win back a loss, rather than because your setup is there. The usual signs are trading straight after a loss, increasing size, taking a trade you can't name a setup for, and pushing past your stop or daily limit. To stop it, make it visible in your journal, decide in advance what you'll do after a loss, and check its cost in dollars every week, so the habit has a number attached.

## What revenge trading looks like

It rarely feels like revenge at the time. It feels like "the market owes me one" or "I'll just get back to flat". Common patterns:

- **A quick re-entry** in the same market, minutes after being stopped out.
- **Bigger size** than usual, to recover the loss in one go.
- **No setup,** or a loose version of one: "close enough".
- **A moved or missing stop,** because another loss feels unbearable.
- **Trading past your limit** for the day, or outside your usual hours.
- **Feelings** you'd describe as frustrated, angry, rushed or desperate.

You don't need all of these. One or two, after a loss, is enough to tag.

## Why it costs more than one bad trade

A revenge trade is usually taken with less care and often at more size, so its losses tend to be larger. It also tends to come in runs: a loss, a revenge trade, a bigger loss, another one. On a prop-firm account, that run is how a normal losing day turns into a breached daily loss limit, or eats the room above a [trailing drawdown](/guides/trailing-drawdown-explained/) floor.

## How to spot revenge trading in your journal

You can't fix what you can't see. Record a few things on every trade:

1. **How you felt**, from a short fixed list that includes a word like "revenge" or "frustrated". Be honest; nobody else reads it.
2. **Whether it was a setup from your playbook,** by name, or "none".
3. **Size compared with your usual,** so oversizing stands out.
4. **Time since your last trade,** and whether that trade lost.

After a few weeks, compare groups of trades. An illustrative example:

| Trades | Count | Average per trade |
|---|---|---|
| Taken within 10 minutes of a losing trade | 14 | −$62 |
| Tagged "frustrated" or "revenge" | 9 | −$95 |
| All other trades | 46 | +$18 |

Figures like these are the strongest argument you'll ever have with yourself. It's your own data, not a rule someone handed you. If you also measure in R, see [how to calculate R multiple](/guides/how-to-calculate-r-multiple/).

## Decide what happens after a loss, before it happens

The moment after a loss is the worst time to make decisions. So make them in advance, in writing. Some examples of the kinds of rules traders set (choose and adapt your own):

- **A pause after a stop-out:** a fixed number of minutes away from the screen before the next trade.
- **A loss count:** stop for the day after a set number of losing trades.
- **A personal daily stop** in dollars, inside any firm's limit.
- **No size increase after a loss.** Size can only go up after a winning day, or not at all mid-session.
- **Setup or nothing:** if you can't name the setup from your playbook, there's no trade.

Make each rule something you can answer yes or no, and put it where you'll see it: on your pre-session checklist, or on a note by the screen. Your [setup playbook](/guides/how-to-write-a-trading-playbook/) is where "setup or nothing" gets its list.

## What to do in the moment

When you notice the urge, run a short routine before you click:

1. **Take your hand off the mouse.** Stand up if you can.
2. **Write one line in your journal:** what you feel and what you want to do.
3. **Check the last trade's result in dollars.** A loss of the size you planned is a planned outcome, not a disaster.
4. **Check your rules.** Has the pause passed? Are you under your loss count? Is this a named setup?
5. **If any answer is no, you're done for now.**

Writing the line is the important part. It turns a feeling into something you can read later, and the pause it creates is often enough.

## Review revenge trades every week

In your [weekly review](/guides/how-to-review-your-trades-weekly/), give revenge trading its own line:

- **How many** trades were tagged, or taken within your pause window after a loss?
- **What did they cost** in dollars, compared with following your rule?
- **What triggered them?** A big loss, a run of small ones, a missed move, a target nearly reached?
- **Did last week's rule hold?** If not, was the rule unclear, or was it too easy to skip?

Change one thing at a time, and measure it. Over a month, check whether the count and the cost fall. If they don't, the rule may need to be simpler or harder to ignore.

## When it's more than a trading habit

If losses, or the urge to trade, are affecting your sleep, your money or the people around you, step back and talk to someone you trust, or to your GP. A journal is useful for spotting patterns, but it's not a substitute for support.
